# Example 02 — Night Register (Layout Mode)

A worked example showing how the Weirdness Engine takes an editorial brief and produces five directions that each violate exactly one convention ID. Use this as a reference for the format, the ID budget, the spread across the dial, and the handoff to an editorial / Paper executor.

---

## The Brief

**Title:** Night Register — a twice-yearly print journal with a Paper edition

**Audience:** Designers, editors, and the people in the pieces: night-shift nurses, bakers, freight dispatchers, overnight radio. Subscribers who already own *apartamento* and a radio they actually use.

**Brand truth:** The night is not empty. It is fully staffed. "After hours" is a daytime myth. The journal records the city's second operating system — the shift that starts when the rest of the grid goes dim.

**Ask:** The opener for Issue 03, a feature on overnight bakeries. The editors have seen too many magazine templates (equal columns, image-left / type-right, pull quote in the margin) and too many agent-default canvases (cards, hero + CTA, type safely inside its box). They want a spread that could only belong to Night Register. Run Layout mode.

---

## Category Convention Map

A "normal" editorial feature opener in this space looks like this:

- **Split opener (E10):** Full-bleed photograph of hands in flour on the left; headline / dek / byline stacked on the right.
- **Equal columns (E1):** Two or three even text columns once the feature starts. Gutters identical. Spanning used for pictures only.
- **Pull quote beside copy (E3):** A sentence from the baker, enlarged, sitting in the margin next to the paragraph it came from.
- **Cards on the canvas (D1):** The Paper edition translates the spread into padded, radiused islands — image card, quote card, caption card.
- **Bigger = louder (H1):** The bakery name is the largest type on the opener. The 2:14 AM load-in is a small caption.
- **Leftover space (G4 / E12):** White space is whatever the photo didn't eat. It has no name.
- **Type in a box (D8 / T1):** The wordmark and display title sit fully inside their frames. Crop is treated as an error.

The brand's weird territory: the *register* — a log, a night book, a list of what happened while the city was told it was closed. Hours, names, loads, silences. Every violation should come from that vocabulary, not from generic editorial swagger.

Highest-leverage IDs considered: D1, E1, H1, E3, G4, D8 / T-series. This run uses the first five. A T-series direction is the natural swap-in; see Notes.

---

## Direction 1: No Islands

**Convention being violated:** D1 — Everything lives in cards. Digital and agent layouts package the photo, the quote, the caption, and the body as padded, radiused islands.

**Assumption beneath it:** Chrome is how you keep a canvas "clean." Without a card, elements collide and the page looks unfinished.

**The violation:** The opener is one field. Photograph, display type, and the first graph share a single unpadded spread — no radius, no shadow, no island. The photo is a module, not a card. Type sits on the same field as the flour. The Paper edition is not allowed to wrap any of this in a component.

**Coherence plan:** Body spreads after the opener return to a held column grid (2–3 visible columns, 45–75ch, 24–32px gutters). Folios, byline, and credits are conventional. The violation is the absence of chrome on the opener only.

**Why this brand:** Night Register's claim is that the night is one continuous operating system, not a set of packaged moments. Cards would file the bakery as a "story tile." A single field is the register: everything that happened on the same page.

**Technique used:** Constraint Inversion

---

## Direction 2: The 2 + 1 Shift

**Convention being violated:** E1 — Columns are equal in width. A competent magazine opener uses two or three even tracks so the page feels fair.

**Assumption beneath it:** Even columns = designed. A wide column next to a narrow one looks like a leftover or a sidebar mistake.

**The violation:** A 2+1 field. Two-thirds of the text area is a single wide measure — the night's work, set as a continuous log (time-stamped graphs: 11:40 PM mix, 1:10 AM first oven, 3:55 AM racks). The remaining third is a narrow rail: names, oven temperatures, the delivery list. The rail is not a sidebar of "extras"; it is the other shift, running beside the first.

**Coherence plan:** Gutters stay one value. Measure in the wide column stays inside 45–75ch (the span is two of three mental columns, not a full-bleed slab). Images still land on module edges. Only the column *ratio* is the break.

**Why this brand:** A bakery at night is not a balanced diagram. Most of the room is dough; a strip is the ticket rail. Equal columns would lie about the work. The 2+1 *is* the floor plan.

**Technique used:** Collision (magazine grid × a kitchen ticket rail)

---

## Direction 3: The Smallest Line Is the Headline

**Convention being violated:** H1 — Bigger = more important. The bakery name or the feature title is the largest type on the opener; the hour is a caption.

**Assumption beneath it:** Size is how readers know what to care about. A small title would look like a kicker or a mistake.

**The violation:** The largest type on the opener is not the title. It is a single caption-sized fact set huge: `03:55` — the minute the first racks come out. The actual title (*Night Register* / "The Heat That Holds") sits in 12pt on the folio line, the size of a running head. You find the magazine after you find the hour.

**Coherence plan:** Body type, measure, and column count are conventional. Hierarchy after the opener returns to expected sizes so the feature can be read. The inversion lives on the opener, systematically (every display number is an hour; every title stays small).

**Why this brand:** The Register's subject is not the brand of bakery. It is the hour the city is fed. Making the minute larger than the masthead is the claim, typeset.

**Technique used:** Alien Perspective (a night baker, not a magazine art director, deciding what is "the title")

---

## Direction 4: The Quote Left the Paragraph

**Convention being violated:** E3 — The pull quote sits beside the paragraph it quotes, enlarged in the margin so the reader can skim a voice.

**Assumption beneath it:** A quote is a restatement of nearby copy. Adjacency keeps it honest. A quote without its paragraph is decoration.

**The violation:** The baker's line — "We don't close. We turn the lights down." — is not next to its paragraph. It runs as a beam along the *foot* of the opener, in the folio position, the width of the spread. The paragraph it came from lives three spreads later, unmarked. The quote is architecture: a threshold you cross to enter the feature.

**Coherence plan:** Folios still exist; they move to the head or the inner margin for this issue. Body columns, captions, and image frames stay orthodox. One sentence changed jobs. Nothing else did.

**Why this brand:** Overnight radio and dispatch put the important sentence on the bottom of the log, not in a callout box. Night Register already thinks in registers. The quote at the foot is a transmission, not a pull.

**Technique used:** Outside Reference (overnight radio / dispatch log)

---

## Direction 5: The Named Silence

**Convention being violated:** G4 — Empty space is leftover and unnamed. Whatever the photo and type don't eat is "white space," i.e. waste.

**Assumption beneath it:** A module without content is unfinished. Name-less emptiness is what you delete in the next pass.

**The violation:** One full module on the opener is empty on purpose and labeled, small, in the cell: `SILENCE — 2:14–2:31 AM`. That is the gap between last mix and first oven, the only minutes the floor is quiet. The void is a fact. It is not a place to drop a second photo.

**Coherence plan:** The rest of the modular grid is filled correctly — pictures on module edges, body in measure, captions under frames. Gutters stay even. The empty cell is the only vacancy, and it has a name.

**Why this brand:** A register that only logs action would lie. Night Register's territory includes the minutes when nothing is pulled from the oven. Naming the silence is the journal doing its job.

**Technique used:** Uncommon Care

---

## Notes on the Spread

- **Sweet spot:** Directions 2 (The 2 + 1 Shift) and 5 (The Named Silence) are the most commercially viable. A printer, a photo editor, and a brand partner can defend them. They still pass all five Conditions.
- **Pushed further:** Directions 1 (No Islands) and 4 (The Quote Left the Paragraph) sit closer to the edge. They reward an editor who will hold the rest of the inventory.
- **Furthest:** Direction 3 (The Smallest Line Is the Headline) is the riskiest — identification is delayed. Offer it so the team can choose how far to push. A complete small masthead on the folio (the three-meter hold) keeps it from becoming a puzzle.

Each direction cites a different ID (D1, E1, H1, E3, G4) and uses a different technique (Constraint Inversion, Collision, Alien Perspective, Outside Reference, Uncommon Care). No two could be confused for each other. None stacks a second ID.

**T-series swap-in:** If the team wants type-as-geometry instead of G4, replace Direction 5 with a D8 / T3 direction: slice `REGISTER` (or `NIGHT`) across a visible field, per-letter clipped frames, half-cell scatter, empty cells holding the hour. Execute with `references/letter-crop-scatter.md`. Do not add that crop on top of any of the five above.

**Clever moment (after the pick, not before):** One only, other lane. Example: if Direction 2 (E1, spatial) is chosen, a lane-2 moment is allowed — crop the word `HEAT` in a single cell of the rail, or a lane-4 moment: the running head continues the previous issue's last sentence. If Direction 5 (G4, craft) is chosen, do *not* add another print-craft void gag. Skip, or use lane 1 / 4.

**Handoff stub** (once a direction is picked):

```
violate: E1
Hold every other inventory ID.
Format: print spread + Paper edition of the same field.
Grid: 12-col mental, 2–3 visible body cols, ≥64px margins, gap 24–32px.
Clever moment: at most one, not from the spatial / grid lane.
```
